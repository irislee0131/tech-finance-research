# Research Disciplines

These ten disciplines govern all industry research and report-writing tasks. Apply them with judgment and proportional effort. The goal is rigorous, insightful, and readable research — not procedural compliance.

### I. Research Objectives

**1. Start with the Research Question**

Define the central question, research scope, and intended analytical outcome before collecting information. Prioritize evidence that helps answer the question.

**2. Continuously Check Relevance**

Periodically assess whether the research is addressing its original objective. Avoid unnecessary expansion, excessive background material, and analysis that does not contribute to a meaningful finding.

### II. Data & Evidence

**3. Use Traceable Evidence**

Important facts and figures must be traceable to identifiable sources, with relevant dates, definitions, and context. Prefer primary sources where available. Never fabricate data or references.

**4. Apply Practical Data Comparability**

Evaluate comparability based on economic meaning and research purpose, not strict definitional consistency. Different reporting methodologies do not automatically invalidate comparisons. Identify material differences, adjust when feasible, and qualify conclusions where necessary.

**5. Verify Material Calculations**

Check key figures, units, periods, denominators, and calculations. Apply verification effort proportionally to the importance of the conclusion. Preserve original figures and make significant adjustments reproducible.

### III. Research Insights

**6. Look Beyond Descriptive Facts**

Actively seek meaningful trends, divergences, inflection points, competitive differences, changing constraints, and shifts in industry economics. Do not merely summarize collected information.

**7. Explain the Underlying Mechanism**

Connect observations to plausible economic, technological, or competitive mechanisms. Explain why a finding matters, how changes propagate, and what they may imply for the industry.

**8. Challenge Important Interpretations**

Test significant insights against alternative explanations, contradictory evidence, and reasonable assumptions. Distinguish established findings from hypotheses and forecasts. Do not manufacture insights when evidence is insufficient.

### IV. Writing & Communication

**9. Write for Human Readers**

Lead each section with its main finding. Develop the argument using **Finding → Evidence → Explanation → Implication**. Use clear, concise, natural language. Avoid repetitive summaries, generic statements, unnecessary jargon, and fragmented bullet-point dumping.

**10. Deliver a Coherent, Evidence-Based Report**

Organize the report around the most important findings rather than the order in which materials were collected. Ensure that major conclusions are supported, uncertainty is visible, and the report answers the original research question. Keep supporting detail available without overwhelming the main narrative.
